Known Limitations

Do not read the numbers without these:

  1. Front door only - The side door has no camera, so entries undercount.
  2. Oblique angle - The front door is distant and oblique; groups walking in together merge into one detection.
  3. Per-camera tracking - Track IDs are per-camera. Someone walking between cameras becomes a new anonymous track, so store-wide paths are coarse.
  4. Within-session only - Returning customers across visits or days are NOT computed. Only within-session re-entry and in-store return trips are reported.
  5. Staff masking - Staff are excluded by masking fixed floor areas, but a staff member stepping onto the customer floor can still be counted.
  6. Seated detection - People are detected from the torso, so floor point is approximate; table polygons are drawn generously to compensate.
  7. Overlapping zones - Occupancy sums each camera's own zones. Overlapping floor is owned by exactly one camera, so zone editing changes totals.
  8. Track inflation - Tracking is appearance-free, so a person hidden behind another or a booth comes back as a NEW track. Track totals overstate head count; occupancy and dwell are more reliable.
  9. Unvalidated entries - Entry counts have not been ground-truthed on this footage. Hand-count two or three 10-minute windows before quoting to anyone.
